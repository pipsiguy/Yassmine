The C-terminal domain (CTD) of RNA Polymerase II is a repeating heptapeptide scaffold whose phosphorylation pattern — the "CTD code" — coordinates every stage of transcription, from initiation through termination, and couples transcription to RNA processing.

In the Schwer Lab at Weill Cornell Medicine, we use budding and fission yeast genetics together with biochemical assays to map how specific CTD modifications direct gene expression, and how their loss perturbs downstream processing of nascent RNA.

Current work includes:

- Genetic screens identifying modifiers of CTD-dependent transcription
- RNA processing assays that link CTD marks to co-transcriptional splicing and 3' end formation
- Cross-talk between CTD signaling and phosphate homeostasis via lncRNA-mediated interference

This line of work aims to produce a mechanistic picture of how a small chemical vocabulary on Pol II controls the flow of genetic information.
